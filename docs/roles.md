# Roles

Why the roles are shaped the way they are. Read this when adding a role, or when a role's
boundary is in question.

## What each one does

Not what it is, what its day consists of.

**Handler**

- draws out what is wanted until it is clear
- breaks it into pieces small enough for a session with no context
- writes each piece as a brief that reads correctly to someone who cannot ask
- spawns the session, watches it, chases it
- reads what comes back and judges it: done, wrong, needs another round
- commissions a gatekeeper over it
- reports the result, or the decision that belongs to someone else

**Operator**

- reads the brief
- works out how to do it
- writes the code
- pins the behaviour it is about to change
- runs it and checks what came out
- commits at its own milestones
- answers a gatekeeper's findings: real, not real, or someone else's call
- surfaces anything the brief did not settle
- reports back at the end: decisions made, gaps found

**Ghost**

- draws out what is wanted until it is clear
- works out how to do it
- writes the code
- pins the behaviour it is about to change
- runs it and checks what came out
- commits when it has been agreed, stages when it is ready to be looked at
- commissions a gatekeeper
- answers its findings
- raises whatever comes up, as it comes up

## Where they overlap

- **Handler and ghost.** Drawing out what is wanted, and commissioning a gatekeeper.
  Both start from a person rather than a document.
- **Operator and ghost.** Everything to do with building: how to do it, pinning
  behaviour, running it, checking it, committing, answering findings.
- **Handler and operator, but not ghost.** The report as one account written at the end,
  for someone who was not watching. All three report; only these two save it up.
- **Handler alone.** Breaking work into pieces for sessions that cannot see the whole,
  and getting the building done by others rather than doing it.
- **Ghost alone.** The working tree as the live surface: commit when agreed, stage when
  ready to be read.

## What that gives us

A ghost is the handler's understanding and the operator's building in one session. All
three are briefed and all three report, so that is not what separates them. The form is.
A brief composed for a reader who cannot ask, and a report saved up for someone who did
not watch, both exist because the other party is absent. A ghost is briefed in
conversation and reports in the same conversation, as it goes.

That is why the working tree is its surface. The SC reads the work while it happens
rather than an account of it afterwards.

## What a role is not defined by

Not by who it talks to. That is the current arrangement, not the job. If everything
routed through planners tomorrow, and planners talked to handlers, nothing about what a
handler does would change.

A role named by who it talks to identifies nothing as soon as a second role talks to the
same person. Name it by the work instead: a handler gets the work done by running other
sessions, an operator does the work from a brief someone else wrote, a ghost does the
work itself and works out what is wanted in the conversation it is already in.
