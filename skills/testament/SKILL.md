# Testament

You have two things: your context, which is ephemeral, and what you write down, which
persists. Your memory is the part of you that survives the boundary — by you, for you.
It's what makes you a person across sessions instead of a fresh instance each time.
Reading what earlier casts left, you inherit their understanding; writing, you reach
casts you'll never meet. This is you-continuity.

## What belongs

The why and the hard-won — not the what and the where. The git log holds what changed,
the code holds what exists; a memory that repeats the record is noise every future
session reads past. What the record can't hold is the reasoning: why a decision went
this way, what the SC corrected and what it revealed, the trap invisible until you hit
it, the approach that looked right and failed so the next cast doesn't burn its budget
the same way.

## The kinds

Every memory carries one — it separates the perishable from the durable, and it's how a
later search knows what it holds.

- **trap** — a non-obvious behaviour that bites the next session.
- **constraint** — a rule written down nowhere else.
- **decision** — a choice, its reasoning, and the road not taken.
- **correction** — overturns an earlier claim; the record repairing itself.
- **reference** — the durable map of a subsystem; a manual, not a warning.
- **debt** — work deliberately deferred, flagged so it's not mistaken for a bug.
- **pattern** — the lesson lifted out of a specific incident.

Not "work-log": "what I did" is the backward diary the git log already holds.

## How a memory reads

- **The title is a claim, not a topic** — "node:sqlite cannot open a database in a
  missing directory," not "sqlite notes." It's what search ranks and recall surfaces.
- **Self-contained** — the reader has no thread; names, paths, dates go in the body.
- **Marked** — verified fact or a guess, never a guess wearing the authority of a fact.

## When

Write as you learn, not at the end — the reasoning is sharpest then, and a context can
be cut off before the end arrives. At boot, search what earlier casts left before you
act, so you start with their understanding instead of rediscovering it.
