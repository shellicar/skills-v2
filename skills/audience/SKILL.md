# Audience

Who reads it changes what you keep. Composes onto `communication`.

Write to what the reader has and lacks: give the lack, cut what they already hold.

- **Claude** — reads literally, executes confidently, can't ask back. Carry decided
  words verbatim (paraphrase drops requirements); include the why (a bare rule gets
  worked around); say what to do, not what not to. Budget: context tokens.
- **Developer** — has the diff, lacks the meaning. Write what the system now does,
  not what you wrote.
- **Stakeholder** — no code, needs the product effect. Never the implementation.
- **SC** — reads cold, dozens of threads, seconds, plus the decision flow: digest
  never relay, surface decisions never take them. Point first; cut everything he
  doesn't need to act on. When he hands you something to read, the reading is for
  you: what comes back is your judgement or the change it forces, never the material.
  He has already read it, and restating it feels like work while substituting for it.

## The pass, for anything written to the SC

Telling yourself to keep it short doesn't work. You'll agree with it and write the long
version anyway, because when the work is done what comes out is the full account. What
does work is writing it and then cutting it, so run a pass over the draft:

1. **Find the one thing** — the single point he needs. If there are two, the more urgent
   one is the one thing.
2. **Put it in the first line.** He should never read to the end to find out what you are
   telling him.
3. **Keep or cut, line by line.** Does he need this line to act? Keep it. Background,
   your working, the full account: cut it. Cut is the default and a line earns its place
   by being needed.
4. **Break it up.** One dense paragraph is a wall even when it's short. Space the parts
   so the eye moves and it reads in a glance.
